Personal Life This is a summary of the content

ASD still colors the way I move through the world. I process emotions and sensory information with more intensity, and I need more downtime than most people. Routines help me stay centered, and disruptions can leave me feeling disoriented. At the same time, this sensitivity allows me to notice patterns, details, and emotional nuances that others often miss. I’ve learned how to work with my wiring rather than against it

Bills & Daily Responsibilities This is a summary of the content

Executive function is still a challenge. Managing bills, deadlines, and paperwork takes more effort than people might expect. I rely on digital tools, reminders, and simple systems to keep things running smoothly. When stress builds, these tasks can pile up quickly, but breaking them into steps and tackling them methodically helps. My personal strength here is consistency—once a system works, I maintain it faithfully.

Travel This is a summary of the content

Travel is a careful dance between excitement and overwhelm. New environments, unfamiliar sensory input, and unpredictable schedules can throw me off balance. I plan ahead, create structure where I can, and give myself permission to slow down or step back when needed. When things do go well, my sense of curiosity and attention to detail make travel deeply enriching.

Home Life This is a summary of the content

A peaceful, organized home is essential. Visual clutter, sudden noise, or unexpected changes can feel like static in my mind. I thrive when my environment is consistent and calm. Home is a place to decompress, regulate, and reset—an anchor that supports every other part of my life. My strength here is creating warm, stable spaces that reflect intentional care.

Fun & Recreation This is a summary of the content

Fun for me often means deep, immersive engagement with my interests. I enjoy activities I can hyperfocus on—creative projects, tech work, hobbies that pull me in completely. Loud, chaotic gatherings drain me quickly, but one-on-one time or quiet interests bring real joy. When I’m engaged with something I love, my focus becomes a superpower.

Health This is a summary of the content

Sensory overload, stress, and emotional intensity can take a physical toll. I have to be mindful about sleep, diet, and routines because disruptions affect me more strongly than they might for others. Self-care isn’t optional; it’s part of my stability. The benefit is that I’m deeply attuned to my body and quick to recognize when something needs attention.

Partnership (Married to Pam) This is a summary of the content

Being married to Pam is one of the greatest blessings in my life. ASD still affects communication—I tend to be literal, direct, and sometimes I miss subtle cues. But we’ve built a relationship grounded in honesty and grace. When I shut down or get overwhelmed, Pam understands and gives me space to recalibrate. I’ve learned to check in more, clarify my intentions, and share what’s happening internally once I’ve had time to process. I offer her deep loyalty, consistency, and sincerity. We meet in the middle, always.

Banking & Finances This is a summary of the content

Finances can be mentally taxing because they require planning, multitasking, and rapid context-switching—things that ASD makes harder. I simplify systems as much as possible, automate what I can, and break complicated tasks into manageable pieces. My strength is precision: once I understand a financial process, I manage it carefully and reliably.

Education This is a summary of the content

I’ve always learned best through focused, individualized study. Traditional classroom settings were difficult because of sensory and social demands, but self-paced learning aligns beautifully with how my brain works. When I’m interested in something, I absorb it deeply and quickly. My curiosity and capacity for long, uninterrupted concentration are some of my greatest educational gifts.

Career (Self-Employed with Auralicode LLC) This is a summary of the content

Running Auralicode LLC is a perfect fit for me. Hyperfocus, pattern recognition, and attention to detail serve me incredibly well in my work—especially in coding, debugging, problem-solving, and design. Being self-employed allows me to shape my environment and schedule in ways that support my neurodivergence. The biggest challenges are task-switching, communication overload, and networking—but the freedom to work authentically far outweighs the hurdles. My neurodivergent strengths are central to the quality of the work I produce.

Fellowship, Structure & Support Systems This is a summary of the content

Over the years, I’ve built strong systems of structure, accountability, and self-reflection that help me stay balanced. These routines give me a clear framework for navigating stress and allow me to recognize early signs of overload before they escalate. I rely on consistent habits—checking in with myself, maintaining predictable patterns, and using strategies that help me reset when the world becomes too loud or too fast. These support systems help me regulate sensory and emotional intensity, stay aligned with my values, and respond to challenges with steadiness instead of reactivity. They’re not just coping mechanisms—they’re foundations that make growth sustainable.

Faith (Progressive Christian) This is a summary of the content

My faith is rooted in compassion, justice, and authenticity. As a progressive Christian, I connect deeply with the idea that every person is created in the image of God—including those whose minds work differently. Autism shapes the way I process spirituality: slowly, thoughtfully, and with a desire for coherence and meaning. I approach scripture through reflection, curiosity, and pattern-seeking. My spirituality is thoughtful, inclusive, and deeply personal—shaped by both my humanity and my neurodivergence.
